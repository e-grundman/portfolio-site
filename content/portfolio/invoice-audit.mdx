Parcel invoices are wrong more often than most shippers know, and the errors are small enough on any one line that nobody chases them. A residential charge on a warehouse address, an additional handling fee on a box that clears every threshold, a billed weight nobody can reproduce from the carton. Audit vendors exist because the arithmetic is tedious and the rules live in a 130-page service guide. They charge a share of what they recover.

The audit is two jobs, and only one of them is arithmetic. Rebuilding what a package should have cost from its dimensions, weight, zone, and address type is a formula. Deciding whether a line is a dispute, a question, or a charge you cannot check from an invoice at all is judgment: a delivery area surcharge at the right amount might be wrong, but proving it takes the carrier's ZIP tables, and a claim you cannot support costs you standing on the next one. The agent above is built around that split. The tools do the arithmetic and quote the rule. The model decides which packages to inspect, which rule governs a line, and what to leave alone.

The invoice is synthetic and the errors are planted, so the run can be scored. That is the point of showing the answer key: an audit that cannot be checked is a vendor pitch. The recorded run is stored with the page, and the live button runs the same agent on a fresh copy of the invoice, with the tool calls shown as they happen.
